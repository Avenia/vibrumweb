I string new acoustics slowly. One string at a time, up to pitch, then left for an hour before the next. The body needs time to register the tension, the neck to settle, the nut slots to find their depth. There is no shortcut that does not cost you tuning stability later.

The Driftwood OM took a full afternoon to string. By the end of it, the D string was holding pitch and the others were close. I played it for the first time just before the light outside went.

## What It Sounds Like

European spruce over walnut is not a common combination in the contemporary market. It should be.

The bass is present but not overwhelming — not the big, round low-end of a spruce-and-rosewood dreadnought. The bass here is controlled, with a clear fundamental and a fast decay that prevents it from muddying the mids. In open-G tuning it rings like a bell.

The midrange is where this guitar lives. The complexity I was hoping for from the European spruce is there: a bloom on sustained notes that builds slightly after the attack and then fades slowly. Chord voicings in the middle register — capo at 2nd fret, open shapes — have a texture that is difficult to describe without playing the instrument.

The treble is clear and slightly warm. The walnut back and sides add brightness without the clinical edge that some maple-backed guitars have. High notes on the first string sustain longer than I expected.

Projection is modest — this is not a guitar for a large room without amplification. It is a guitar for a small room, close listening, or a microphone at 30cm. The client plays at home and records. It is correct for both.

## Setup

Final action at the 12th fret: 2.3mm bass, 1.8mm treble. Nut slots cut to standard acoustic depth — no binding, no buzzing. Intonation set with compensated saddle, B-string pulled back 1.1mm from theoretical.

Tuners are Gotoh 510 — open gear, gold, the finish closest to the Spanish cedar headstock overlay. They hold pitch without being stiff.

## Sending It Out

The client came on a Tuesday morning. He does not play much lead — he plays chords, he sings, he records alone at night. He sat with the Driftwood OM for half an hour without saying anything significant. He asked about the wood. I told him about the spruce from Tara, the woodworker in Novi Sad, the two years it sat waiting.

He said it sounded like it had been waiting.

He was not wrong.
