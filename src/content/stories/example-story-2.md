Most of the guitars I build sit somewhere in the vintage-adjacent spectrum — light bodies, bright finishes, single-coils with some air in them. The Example Guitar 2 is not that guitar.

The client came in with a reference folder and one sentence: *something that sounds like the room getting smaller.* We spent an hour talking through what that actually meant before I touched any wood.

## Body and Wood Choice

The body is African mahogany — a single piece, about 4.8kg before routing. Heavier than I usually like, but the brief called for density and sustain over resonance and liveliness. Chambering was considered and rejected. We wanted mass.

The top is a bookmatched set of figured walnut, 6mm thick, glued and levelled before the body was profiled. The grain runs parallel across both halves — the figure is subtle, more texture than flash. Under a dark oil finish it reads almost black in shadow and dark brown in direct light.

Carve is a modified LP-style belly cut, softer radius than standard. The belly cut on a heavy guitar matters more than people think.

## Neck

One-piece roasted maple, oil finish only. No lacquer on the neck — the client specifically asked for this, and after trying both in the same session he was right that the raw wood feels better under fast movement.

Scale is 628mm — slightly longer than Fender standard, slightly shorter than baritone. It gives the low strings a little more tension without pushing the whole instrument into baritone territory.

Frets are Jescar 6105 stainless. Last time I used stainless on a dark-toned build, the owner came back six months later to say he had not touched the fret polish once. Good enough reason.

Fingerboard is ebony. Real ebony, the kind that is getting harder to source — straight, dense, almost no grain variation. It adds a certain stiffness to the attack on every note that complements the mahogany body better than rosewood would.

## Hardware and Electronics

Locking tuners, matte black. Bridge is a Hipshot hardtail — fixed, no tremolo. The client does not use vibrato and did not want the weight or complexity of a trem cavity.

Pickups are a pair of Bare Knuckle Mules, neck and bridge. The Mule is a PAF-style wind but with a bit more steel in the midrange — not modern-high-output, not vintage-soft, somewhere between. Run clean they are detailed enough to use. Run with gain they do not turn to mush.

Volume and tone controls only. No coil splits, no phase switches, no push-pull complications. The brief was directness.

## Where It Is Now

The body is shaped and the top is glued. Neck pocket is routed and fitting well — the neck drops in with firm hand pressure and no lateral play. Finishing starts next week.

Dark builds always make the finishing stage feel heavier than it is. There is less margin for a blemish to disappear into the character of the wood. Everything shows.

That is the point.
