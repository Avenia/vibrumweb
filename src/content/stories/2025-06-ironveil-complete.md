The walnut top looks different every time the light changes. This morning it was almost black. An hour ago it caught the window and showed every grain line in amber. The oil finish does this — it does not coat the wood, it reveals it.

The Ironveil is done.

## Finishing Notes

The body received three coats of Rubio Monocoat oil, hand-applied and buffed between coats. No stain — the walnut and mahogany have enough colour of their own. The contrast between the two woods is visible at the binding line: the walnut top is warmer, the mahogany back a few shades cooler.

The neck got a single oil coat and was left alone. After a week of handling it feels broken-in in a way that a lacquer neck takes months to get to.

## Fretwork

The Jescar 6105 stainless frets were levelled, crowned, and polished over two sessions. Stainless takes longer to level than nickel silver — the file loads faster and you have to clean it constantly — but the end result is a fret surface that will outlast the guitar if the guitar is treated reasonably.

Final action: 1.7mm bass, 1.5mm treble at the 12th. The longer scale and heavier strings (11–52) give the low end exactly the resistance the client wanted. It does not feel slack. It does not feel brutal. It feels intentional.

## Sound

I played it for about two hours before packing it. That is longer than my usual pre-delivery session — I was curious about something.

The Bare Knuckle Mules, in a mahogany body with an ebony board, are more articulate than I expected. There is a midrange push that can go harsh if you push the amp, but through a clean Fender-style circuit they separate notes in a chord clearly. Run with a small amount of drive they are exactly what the brief described: the room getting smaller, but each note still audible within it.

The client played it for three hours when he came to pick it up. He found one setting — bridge pickup, tone at 7, just enough drive to compress the attack slightly — and did not move from it.

That is the right outcome.

## What I Would Change

The belly cut on the back could be slightly deeper. At 4.8kg the guitar is heavy on a strap, and an extra 2mm of carve would have helped the balance point without affecting the tone meaningfully. I filed this away for the next dense-body build.

The neck heel could be slightly shorter at the 14th fret access. The geometry is fine, but a tighter heel joint would look cleaner from the back. Minor.

Both are things only I will notice.
