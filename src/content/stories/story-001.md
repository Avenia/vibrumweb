Two acoustic guitars, built side by side on the same bench. Both are small jumbos with the same outline and the same neck. One is walnut, the other mahogany, and the mahogany one has a cutaway. The tops are the finest spruce from Serbia. The first guitar has back and sides of walnut, also from Serbia, picked out piece by piece. The second has mahogany.

The two woods give two voices. Walnut gives more bass: a full, rich sound. Mahogany pushes the midrange forward and gives more overtones. Both work on stage and both work in the studio. Which one is the right one is a matter of taste.

The necks are laminated from maple and mahogany: strips glued into one blank that stays straighter than a single piece ever would.

There is as little hardware on these guitars as we could get away with: tuners, a bone nut, truss rod, frets and saddle. Nothing else. Nothing screwed, nothing bolted.
