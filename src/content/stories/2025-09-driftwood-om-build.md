The Driftwood OM has a closed body now.

This sounds simple. It is not simple. Closing the box on an acoustic guitar is the point of no return — everything inside is permanent. Every brace, every kerfing strip, every glue joint. If something is wrong, you find out later, when finding out means taking a chisel to a finished instrument.

I trusted my work. The back went on clean.

## Bracing

The X-brace pattern I described in the first entry went in without drama. The forward placement of the X-joint — about 3mm ahead of standard — opens up the bass response on a spruce top that might otherwise be too polite. Combined with the scalloped braces (2.9mm at the base, 2.3mm at the waist) the top has a flex that tap-tests well: clear fundamental, long decay, no dead spots near the soundhole.

Finger braces are tapered to thin ends — I do this slowly, over multiple sessions, checking tap tone after each removal of material. It is tedious. It is the part of acoustic building that most directly determines the final sound. I do not rush it.

The back received traditional ladder bracing, four bars, slightly heavier than the top bracing. The walnut back is stiff enough that it does not need the light touch the top requires.

## Binding and Purfling

The body binding is maple — bright enough to separate the top from the sides visually, neutral enough not to compete with the walnut's grain. Purfling is a simple black/white/black strip inside the binding on the top only.

I cut the binding channels by hand with a router plane, not a Dremel tool. It takes longer. The result is cleaner.

## Neck Fit

The dovetail neck joint is fitted but not glued. At this stage the neck needs to come on and off multiple times for alignment checks — I will glue it after finishing.

The neck heel sits flush against the body with no gap visible on either side. Neck angle is 1.5 degrees, which should give correct saddle height on a standard bridge with the top and bracing I have. This was calculated before building and verified three times during the process. Acoustic neck angles done wrong cannot be corrected. They just produce wrong guitars.

## The Sound Already

Before the back went on I played the top like a dulcimer — held against my chest, dampened with one hand, tapped and strummed with the other. It is a strange thing to do in a workshop. It tells you something.

The European spruce top sounds like I hoped: responsive, complex in the midrange, not shrill. The bracing is in the right place. The wood is telling the truth.

Six weeks to stringing is now three weeks to stringing. The finishing will go quickly — hand-rubbed oil finish on the back and sides, shellac on the top. No thick lacquer. This guitar should breathe.
